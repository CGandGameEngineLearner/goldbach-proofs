    Given an even number, where are its two primes?

    There are three ways to make 30 from two primes. They are 7 + 23, 11 + 19 and 13 + 17. Can we always find at least one pair for any even number greater than 2?

    From Hua Luogeng’s book to Chen Jingrun’s “1 + 2”

    In Additive Theory of Prime Numbers, Hua Luogeng studied how to write integers as sums of primes and their powers. As a young mathematician in Xiamen, Chen Jingrun improved a result in the book on Tarry’s problem. Hua read his paper and recommended him. In 1957 Chen joined the Institute of Mathematics at the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

    Chen announced his “1 + 2” theorem in 1966 and published the full proof in 1973. Every sufficiently large even number is a prime plus either a prime or a product of two primes. To reach “1 + 1”, we still need to show that we can always choose a representation in which the second summand is itself prime.

    Academy biography (Chinese)Chen’s 1973 paper (Chinese)

    The 1979 lecture in Britain

    Tsinghua University’s history museum dates the lecture to 8 October 1979 and gives its title in Chinese as 《为百万人的数学》. Hua’s assistant Ji Lei recalled the poster for a British lecture as 《百万人的数学》. Ji also described their work in Datong, where the team helped railway workers cut queues for water, ash removal and coal. Practical projects like this occupied nearly two decades.

    Tsinghua’s account (Chinese)Ji Lei’s recollections (Chinese)Hua’s work in factories and farms

    How far does the Riemann hypothesis narrow the search?

    The zeros of the Riemann zeta function tell us how far the count of primes can stray from its predicted value. The generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) for Dirichlet L-functions gives estimates even when we group primes by their remainder modulo a chosen integer.

    In August 2026, Chirre and Hagen proved that, assuming RH, every interval (x, x + 123(ln x)²] with x ≥ 2 contains at least one even number that is a sum of two primes. Now pick a particular even number N in that interval. We still need a prime p for which N − p is also prime. Our research tasks ask how to bound that pair count from below.

      What did the Lean community verify?

      On 20 November, Levent Alpöge released a formal proof developed with Claude. The following day, the Lean and Mathlib communities confirmed that the statement matched Mathlib’s Riemann hypothesis and that multiple proof checkers accepted the proof.

      Nature news reportCommunity verification statementMIT verification record

    2. 13 Nov 2026

      A conditional route to binary Goldbach

      In a manuscript posted by Shandong University, Jiamin Li and Jianya Liu derive prime-pair representations for every sufficiently large even number. They assume Dirichlet GRH together with either of two additional hypotheses on a spectrum. Establishing one of those extra hypotheses remains a step on this route.

      Shandong University abstractAuthors’ manuscript (PDF)

    3. 1 Jun 2026

      Refining “1 + 2” to “1 + 1.9”

      Li and Liu’s preprint gives a sharper form of Chen’s decomposition for sufficiently large even N. Write N = p + rq, where p and q are primes and r is 1 or a prime. Their bound on the smaller factor is r ≤ q0.9. A representation with r = 1 gives a sum of two primes.

      arXiv preprintXiamen University lecture report (Chinese)
